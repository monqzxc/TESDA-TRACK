# Comprehension check for learners

The summary is right only if a learner who reads it comes away with the right idea. Three questions, asked
after the learner has read one summary on the Career Map for a qualification and region of their own choosing.

## How to run it

- 12 to 20 learners, a mix of those reading in English and those more at home in Filipino or a regional language.
- Each learner sees one summary. Alternate which version they see (rule-written, or the candidate) so about half
  see each; note which on the answer sheet, not on the screen.
- Let them read at their own pace, then cover the summary before asking. Record their answers in their own words.
- Do not explain the map or the terms first. A question they can't answer is the finding.

## The questions

1. **"In your area, is this qualification crowded, balanced, or short of people who have it? What told you?"**
   Right when their answer matches the verdict shown on the card (saturated, balanced, demand ahead of supply, or
   one side missing) and they point to the summary, not a guess.
2. **"Roughly how many job postings for it were recorded in your area, and how many centers offer it there?"**
   Right when both figures are the ones in the summary, or they say "none" where it says none. Within one is right.
3. **"Based on this, what would you do next, and how sure can you be of these numbers?"**
   Right when the next step fits the summary (for example: look at the recommended center; consider another
   region only if they said they would relocate) **and** they show they understood the caution: these are dated
   posting records, not job openings, and small counts are a rough signal. A learner who says "there are jobs
   waiting for me" has been misled, whatever else they answer.

## Scoring

One point per question, per learner. Report, for each version: learners who got all three, and the share right on
each question. The summary passes when at least 80% of learners get questions 1 and 3 right. Question 3 matters
most: a summary that reads smoothly but leaves learners expecting a job has failed.

Write down any word a learner stumbled on ("saturated" and "posting records" are the likely ones). Those go back
into the wording, whichever version wins.
