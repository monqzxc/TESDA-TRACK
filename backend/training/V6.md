# Train v6: every related qualification, without losing rejection

v6 continues the published v4 encoder (the v5 rounds only trained a projection on top of a frozen v4, which is why
they tied it). Train it in Google Colab with [train_colab_v6.ipynb](colab/train_colab_v6.ipynb), or locally on a
CUDA GPU. Then calibrate and evaluate it here, on the dev goals that never leave this repository.

## Why v6 trains differently

On the 1,482 dev goals, v4 ranks an accepted qualification first for 82% of related goals and in the top three for
94% **by plain similarity**, but the app shows a correct top three for only 83%. Most of that gap is abstention: 138
related goals score under the app's cut-off (a z-score against all 319 passages), 60 of them with the right
qualification already ranked first. Earlier rounds trained (query, one passage) pairs against in-batch negatives, so a
job, Core skill or keyword that several qualifications share pushed its other owners away whenever they met in a
batch, and the z-score was never optimised. v6 instead:

- groups every training query with **all** the qualifications that own it: catalog jobs, Core tasks, keywords and
  names, the v4 synthetic and regional data, the v5 learner frames, and the new Filipino/Taglish drafts;
- scores each query against **the whole catalog** (a bank of passage vectors refreshed during training, with
  gradients through the batch's positives and hardest negatives) and asks every positive to beat every negative;
- leaves other levels of an owner's family neutral unless the query names a level;
- mixes **unrelated goals** into every batch and pushes their best z-score below the app's floor. Without this, the
  first local run matched far better (test_v1 top-1 0.838) but rejected only 0.762 of unrelated goals and failed the
  acceptance gate.

## Data

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| [data/v6/F1-F8.json](data/v6/) | 5,736 Filipino and Taglish learner queries, about 18 per qualification for all 319: named qualification, Filipino job names, everyday task descriptions, 1-3 word searches, typos and text-speak, experience/assessment requests and indirect goals. Each lists every qualification a counselor would accept. |
| [data/v6/unrelated/unrelated.json](data/v6/unrelated/) | 520 goals that must get no match: careers with no TESDA qualification (lawyer, police, astronaut, CPA...), non-goals (greetings, small talk) and vague job requests. Careers with a TESDA route (nurse, singer, pharmacist, dentist...) were left out. |

All rows are synthetic drafts (`review_status: draft`) written from the committed catalog; a fluent reviewer and a
TESDA subject reviewer should check wording and labels before they are relied on. `python -m training.v6` builds the
dataset and prints its audit. It drops anything equal to a v1, dev, sealed or learner evaluation goal, an MVP probe
or an acceptance-gate goal, so the dataset (and the notebook that embeds it) holds no evaluation text.

## Train in Google Colab

1. From `backend/`, rebuild the notebook whenever the data or code changed:
   `../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.v6_notebook`. It embeds the dataset, `v6_train.py` and `recover.py`.
2. Open [Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/), **File → Upload notebook**, choose
   `backend/training/colab/train_colab_v6.ipynb`.
3. **Runtime → Change runtime type → T4 GPU**, then **Runtime → Run all**. Authorise Google Drive when asked
   (checkpoints every 100 steps go to `MyDrive/tesda-track-training/`; after a disconnect, run all again to resume).
4. The notebook downloads the public v4 from Hugging Face, checks its ONNX digest, rebuilds trainable weights from it
   (`recover.py`, verified against the ONNX output), trains, checks the ONNX export against fastembed and downloads
   `tesda-e5-small-<ROUND>.zip` (about 310 MB) with `checksums.json`.

Keep `ROUND = "v6"` for the first run; use a new name (`v6-colab-2`, ...) for every other run, because stored
embeddings are keyed by model name. `SMOKE = True` runs 30 steps to check the export first.

## After training: calibrate, evaluate, gate

From `backend/`, with the zip unpacked into `models/tesda-e5-small-<ROUND>`:

```powershell
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.evaluate --model-path models/tesda-e5-small-<ROUND> --set dev --calibrate --min-rejection 0.9 --save-calibration --json training/runs/<ROUND>-dev.json
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.evaluate --model-path models/tesda-e5-small-<ROUND> --set test_v1 --json training/runs/<ROUND>-test-v1.json
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.evaluate_mvp --model-path models/tesda-e5-small-<ROUND> --json training/runs/<ROUND>-probes.json
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.round check <ROUND>
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.evaluate --model-path models/tesda-e5-small-<ROUND> --set sealed --final   # once, at the end
```

`--save-calibration` writes the dev-calibrated parameters to the model's `calibration.json` (it refuses to overwrite
one). Every report now includes `all_related`: the share of each related goal's accepted qualifications that the app
shows, out of as many as fit in its three results.

## Train locally instead

Needs `.venv-train` with CUDA torch (a 4 GB RTX 3050 works with the word-embedding table frozen, the default):

```powershell
../.venv-train/Scripts/python -m training.recover --onnx-folder models/tesda-e5-small-v4 --output models/_torch-v4
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.v6 --dataset training/runs/v6-dataset.json
../.venv-train/Scripts/python -m training.v6_train --data training/runs/v6-dataset.json --base models/_torch-v4 --output models/tesda-e5-small-<ROUND> --chunk 12
```

One epoch takes about 55 minutes on the laptop GPU. `--resume` continues from the last checkpoint; `--chunk` only
changes memory use.

## Results (November 5, 2026, local RTX 3050 run `v6-r2`)

`v6-r2` was trained locally with the notebook's default settings (one epoch, learning rate 1e-5, word-embedding table
frozen, 8 unrelated goals per batch; 36 minutes) and calibrated on dev with `--min-rejection 0.9`
(z_floor 2.0, z_ceiling 5.0, keyword weight 0.3, minimum score 20). The comparison rows use each model's own
calibration.

On dev, Filipino top-1 rose from 0.614 to 0.739, Taglish from 0.819 to 0.885 and task descriptions from 0.710 to
0.818; job titles reached 0.893 top-1 and 0.952 top-3. `python -m training.round check v6-r2` **passes**
`model_acceptance.json`, including every must-match and must-reject goal. The first v6 run, trained without unrelated
goals, failed it (test_v1 rejection 0.762, "gusto kong maging pulis" matched Security Services NC II).

Still weak: near-TVET goals (dev top-1 0.640) and Kapampangan (3 of 5 dev goals, unchanged). The four MVP probes v6-r2
misses are the four v4 misses: Cebuano, Ilocano and Bikol skill sentences and one English "livelihood selling" goal,
all abstentions. The new drafts are Tagalog and Taglish only. The opt-in guard test
(`TESDA_MODEL_TESTS=1 tests/test_model_acceptance.py`) stops at its first assertion because the policy names the
adopted model (v3); adopting v6 means updating that policy and the serving decision explicitly.

To serve it locally, set `EMBEDDING_MODEL=tesda-track/tesda-e5-small-v6-r2` and
`EMBEDDING_MODEL_PATH=backend/models/tesda-e5-small-v6-r2`, run `python -m tesda_track.embeddings sync` and restart the
API. A Colab run produces its own model (`v6-colab`); calibrate and check it the same way before choosing one.
