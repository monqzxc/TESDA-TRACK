# Train v5 on demand jobs

## Multilingual MVP revision

The revised recipe is in [train_colab_v5_mvp.ipynb](colab/train_colab_v5_mvp.ipynb).
It reconstructs the demand-trained v5 projection and continues on learner queries for goals,
qualifications, jobs and skills. The encoder remains frozen while the shared projection is trained.
The notebook exports a separate `tesda-e5-small-v5-mvp` folder.

The corpus includes English, Filipino, Taglish, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Ilocano, Kapampangan,
Waray, Bikol and Pangasinan. Many examples retain English trade terms. Regional wording
is marked as draft and needs fluent-speaker review. The 50 independent probes cover these
ten varieties and include ten unrelated requests. Their small size supports regression checking.

After mapping and holdout filtering, 2,134 learner requests join the 55 demand titles.
Ten demand titles stay held out. Learner examples for those titles' qualifications are excluded
to protect that comparison. Exact development, sealed and MVP-probe texts are excluded.

From `backend/`, try any question with the trained model and the app's matching rules:

```powershell
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.try_mvp --query "I want to work in hotel"
# Keep entering questions interactively.
../.venv/Scripts/python -m training.try_mvp
```

This runner uses the committed catalog and loads the model once. It works without the database.
The shared app matcher now recognizes broad hotel employment requests and offers Housekeeping
NC II, Food and Beverage Services NC II and Front Office Services NC II, with role explanations.
Literal qualification codes, catalog jobs and Core skill descriptions also supply matching evidence.

To rebuild the MVP notebook, use `python -m training.v5_notebook --mvp`.
To measure the fixed probes, use `python -m training.evaluate_mvp --model-path models/tesda-e5-small-v5-mvp`.
The November 5 run completed locally and in Colab. It retrieved the expected first qualification
for 36 of 40 learner probes and rejected all ten unrelated questions. The four misses were
abstentions. This ties the original v5 probe results, so additional data has not yet demonstrated
an accuracy gain on that set. Both runs retained all ten held-out demand-title matches.
Training and export checks verify reproducibility and model compatibility, while regional-language
quality still needs fluent-speaker evaluation.

With the shared matcher and inherited scoring settings, the revised model scores 77.63% top-one,
83.01% top-three and 97.99% unrelated-goal rejection on the 1,482 development goals.
The local API and database were offline during validation. The direct model runner and
matching/data tests passed, and full database-backed endpoint validation remains pending.

Serving the model through the full app requires setting its distinct model name and folder,
running `python -m tesda_track.embeddings sync` against the app database, and restarting the API.

Open [Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/), upload
[train_colab_v5.ipynb](colab/train_colab_v5.ipynb), and select **Run all** on a standard CPU runtime.
The notebook includes the training code, filtered public titles and catalog passages, and fetches the
[v4 parent](https://huggingface.co/Nyanta19/tesda-e5-small-v4). It checks the parent ONNX digest before
training. The last cell downloads a ZIP containing the v5 serving folder and its metrics in
`training.json`. If that download is interrupted, rerun the last cell while the runtime is connected.
After a runtime reset, rerun all cells to reproduce the short training job.

This is a job-title-to-qualification matcher, not a vacancy or headcount forecast. It keeps the v4
encoder frozen and fits a 384 × 384 linear projection for queries and passages. The projection starts
at identity, is regularized toward v4, and is folded into the ONNX graph for the existing API.
It optimizes cross-entropy against all 319 qualifications, sharing positive probability across
known owners. Posting frequency affects training weight, capped at four times the base weight.
The inherited v4 scoring parameters allow a fixed-threshold comparison.

The [demand snapshot](../seed/sources/labor_demand/README.md) covers one month of PhilJobNet postings,
national Career Info PH profiles and CALABARZON priority occupations. A title must match a catalog
possible job or a listed role equivalent in [demand.py](demand.py). Employer names, salaries,
individual profiles and unlabeled titles do not enter the notebook. Multiple owners are retained.
Titles are deduplicated across sources and regions; aliases and their canonical title stay on the same
side of the split. Evaluation exclusions are applied before building the notebook.

In the November 4 run, 55 of 985 observed titles survived mapping and exclusions; those titles accounted
for 831 of 4,237 PhilJobNet postings. V5 trained on 45 titles for 40 updates. V4 and v5 both retrieved
a labeled qualification first for all 10 held-out titles. Held-out means unseen during **this
continuation**: catalog titles may already have appeared in v4's training. These are weak labels,
not expert judgments, and the score does not cover unmapped jobs or prove better matching.

On the same 1,482 development goals, using v4's scoring settings for both models:

| Metric | v4 | v5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| First result correct (1,283 related goals) | 77.86% | 77.63% |
| Correct result in top three | 82.85% | 83.09% |
| No result for an unrelated goal (199 goals) | 97.99% | 97.49% |

V5 recovered three extra top-three matches, lost three first-place matches and returned a result
for one extra unrelated goal. Keep this run as a candidate; the sealed set was not used.

The hosted Colab run used the same dataset digest as the local run
(`166f75faff6f01acfde0659428562e23ba5b5f0539e85b17d865582e2d509bbb`). Its training loss fell from
0.278 to 0.029, and the minimum cosine similarity in its ONNX export check was 0.99999994.

To rebuild the notebook from the repository and train locally, from `backend/`:

```powershell
../.venv-train/Scripts/python -m training.v5_notebook
../.venv-train/Scripts/python -m training.v5 --base models/tesda-e5-small-v4 --data training/runs/v5-demand.json --output models/tesda-e5-small-v5
```

Use a new output folder for each run. To compare app-level retrieval, run
`python -m training.evaluate --set dev --model-path models/tesda-e5-small-v5` and repeat for v4.
Evaluate independently labeled employer titles, including no-match jobs, before considering promotion.
